# LCIAnalysis

## From fragmented process data to transparent, connected and scientifically defensible life cycle inventories

**An integrated scientific platform for life cycle inventory development, collaborative data management, process modeling, material and energy balances, and system integration.**

**Version:** 2.0.0 — under release preparation  
**Developed in the context of:** Life Cycle Engineering / LCE SMART, University of Southern Denmark  
**Application:** Windows desktop software  
**Target users:** LCA practitioners, researchers, industrial partners, technology developers and multidisciplinary research consortia

---

## 1. Why LCIAnalysis?

### The challenge: Collecting data is not the same as developing a reliable life cycle inventory

Life cycle assessment (LCA) is increasingly applied to emerging technologies, industrial processes, bio-based products, food systems and circular economy solutions.

However, one of the most challenging and resource-intensive stages of an LCA study is often the development of the underlying **life cycle inventory (LCI)**.

This challenge becomes particularly significant in large collaborative research projects, where several universities, research institutions, technology developers and industrial partners contribute data from different processes, facilities, experimental campaigns and technology development stages.

In such projects, LCA practitioners frequently receive:

- Multiple Excel workbooks prepared by different data providers.
- Process descriptions with inconsistent levels of detail.
- Material inputs and outputs reported in different units or reference bases.
- Incomplete information about energy consumption, process yields, waste streams and operating conditions.
- Process inventories that do not agree with their corresponding mass or energy balances.
- Missing information about the origin, assumptions and quality of reported data.
- Unclear connections between upstream and downstream unit operations.
- Revised datasets that are difficult to compare with earlier submissions.
- Scientific questions and clarifications distributed across emails, meetings and spreadsheet comments.

As the number of project partners, process steps and inventory revisions increases, the complexity of managing these datasets grows rapidly.

**The result is a substantial burden on LCA practitioners**, who must not only calculate environmental impacts but also coordinate data collection, interpret engineering information, identify inconsistencies, communicate with data providers, document assumptions and reconstruct complete production systems.

Traditional spreadsheet-based workflows can support individual calculations, but they often struggle to provide an integrated, transparent and manageable environment for this broader scientific process.

### The LCIAnalysis approach

**LCIAnalysis is designed to bridge the gap between raw engineering data and structured, reviewable life cycle inventories.**

Rather than treating inventory collection, process modeling, data validation, scientific communication and system integration as separate activities, LCIAnalysis brings them into a connected working environment.

Its purpose is to help researchers move systematically from fragmented datasets toward a coherent representation of industrial and emerging production systems.

The platform supports the development of inventories that are easier to understand, verify, revise, communicate and subsequently use in LCA studies.

## 2. What makes LCIAnalysis different?

### More than an Excel inventory tool

LCIAnalysis combines several activities that are commonly handled using separate spreadsheets, drawing applications, calculation files and communication channels.

| Conventional LCI development challenges | How LCIAnalysis helps |
|---|---|
| Different partners submit data in different structures | Structured Excel inventory templates and organized import workflows |
| Large inventories are difficult to interpret | Process-level inventory review and graphical process representation |
| Material flows between processes are unclear | Stream mapping and interprocess connectivity assessment |
| Missing data are discovered late | Structured review of missing, incomplete and inconsistent information |
| Material inputs and outputs do not reconcile | Process-level and system-level mass-balance calculations |
| Energy information is fragmented | Organized energy inventory and energy-balance workflows |
| Scientific questions are scattered across emails | Dedicated query and communication records |
| Revised inventories are difficult to follow | Revision-oriented workflows and documented clarification history |
| Multiple production systems must be combined | System integration, scaling and multi-product modeling workflows |
| Scientific results are difficult to share | Structured Excel, diagram and calculation-report exports |

**The value of LCIAnalysis lies in connecting these activities within a single scientific workflow.**

A missing material stream identified during mass-balance assessment can become a scientific query. A clarification received from a data provider can inform a revised inventory. Updated process information can then be reflected in the process model, calculations and exported documentation.

This connection between data, engineering calculations and scientific communication is particularly valuable in long-running collaborative research projects.

## 3. Designed for collaborative research consortia

### Supporting the complete data-development cycle

Large research consortia often involve multiple work packages and organizations responsible for different parts of a production value chain.

For example, a project developing an alternative protein or fermentation-derived ingredient may include separate partners working on feedstock preparation, fermentation, separation, purification, product formulation and downstream applications.

Each partner may have detailed knowledge of their own technology but limited information about the overall production system.

Meanwhile, the LCA team must assemble the individual datasets into a consistent and interconnected inventory.

LCIAnalysis is designed to support this coordination.

### 3.1 Structured data collection across partners

LCIAnalysis provides a standardized approach to preparing and managing process inventory workbooks.

Instead of starting each data-collection exercise with a different spreadsheet structure, the LCA team can use a consistent framework for requesting material inputs, outputs, energy requirements and supporting process information.

This helps reduce ambiguity and makes datasets easier to review and compare.

### 3.2 Scientific review rather than simple data acceptance

Information received from a technology developer should not automatically be treated as a complete or validated life cycle inventory.

LCIAnalysis supports a review process in which reported quantities, units, process connections, assumptions and calculated balances can be examined before the inventory is used in subsequent modeling.

This is especially important for emerging technologies, where datasets may represent laboratory experiments, pilot-scale operations, engineering estimates or projected industrial systems.

### 3.3 Managing scientific questions and partner responses

One of the most time-consuming aspects of collaborative LCI development is communicating with data providers.

Typical questions include:

- What is the reference basis for the reported material consumption?
- Does a reported stream represent a measured value or an engineering estimate?
- Where does the unaccounted material leave the process?
- Is the reported electricity demand specific to the equipment or the entire facility?
- Are the process yield and final product quantity consistent?
- Has a wastewater or cleaning stream been excluded?
- Which dataset should be considered the latest approved version?

LCIAnalysis provides a structured environment for documenting such questions and following the clarification process.

This helps preserve the scientific reasoning behind inventory corrections and reduces dependence on fragmented email histories.

### 3.4 Supporting continuity across project stages

Research projects often evolve from early laboratory data toward pilot demonstrations and industrial-scale projections.

As technologies mature, process assumptions, operating parameters and inventory values may change.

A structured approach to revisions, source documentation and process-level assessment helps researchers maintain continuity between successive data-development stages.

### 3.5 Improving transparency between work packages

By connecting inventory tables with process diagrams, material and energy calculations, and communication records, LCIAnalysis helps make the relationship between individual work packages and the overall production system more visible.

This can improve technical discussions between the LCA team and the engineers or scientists responsible for each process.

## 4. An integrated scientific workflow

**From raw data to connected inventory systems**

**Step 1 — Define the inventory structure**

Prepare structured Excel templates and organize process-level data collection.

**Step 2 — Import and inspect the datasets**

Review the submitted inventory, identify missing information and examine units, quantities and process descriptions.

**Step 3 — Build the process flow model**

Represent the production system through unit operations and material streams.

**Step 4 — Establish process connectivity**

Map the relationships between upstream outputs and downstream inputs, including the consistency of reported quantities and reference bases.

**Step 5 — Assess mass balance**

Calculate and inspect material inputs, outputs and residuals to identify possible inconsistencies or missing flows.

**Step 6 — Review energy requirements**

Organize electricity, heating, cooling and fuel consumption, and develop the basis for energy-balance and heat-integration analysis.

**Step 7 — Communicate scientific queries**

Document uncertainties, inconsistencies and requests for clarification, and incorporate reviewed updates into the inventory.

**Step 8 — Integrate and scale production systems**

Connect process datasets, adjust reference bases and evaluate integrated production configurations.

**Step 9 — Export and document**

Prepare reviewable inventory workbooks, process diagrams, calculation tables and supporting reports for collaboration and subsequent LCA work.

The workflow is iterative: scientific review may reveal missing information, and new information may require revisions to earlier calculations or process connections.

## 5. Core scientific and technical capabilities

### 5.1 Structured life cycle inventory development

LCIAnalysis supports the preparation, import and review of structured Excel-based life cycle inventories.

The objective is to improve the organization and interpretability of primary process data before their use in environmental assessment.

This is particularly useful when the inventory includes many unit operations, extensive material lists, intermediate products and several categories of energy consumption.

### 5.2 Process flow diagrams and stream mapping

A numerical inventory does not always clearly communicate how a production system operates.

LCIAnalysis provides graphical representations of process sequences and connections, helping users examine how materials move between unit operations.

These visualizations support both engineering interpretation and communication with technology developers.

Stream connectivity assessment is particularly important when different project partners provide upstream and downstream inventories independently.

### 5.3 Mass-balance assessment and calculation transparency

Mass conservation is a fundamental principle of process engineering.

LCIAnalysis supports the assessment of reported material inputs and outputs and the identification of unexplained differences.

Rather than presenting only a numerical result, the tool is designed to make the underlying calculations and assumptions accessible for scientific review.

Its process-level and aggregated balance presentations help researchers investigate inconsistencies and communicate findings with data providers.

A successful numerical balance is an important consistency check, but it does not by itself establish that an inventory is complete or scientifically validated.

### 5.4 Energy inventory and energy-balance analysis

Energy consumption is often a major contributor to the environmental performance of industrial and biotechnological processes.

LCIAnalysis provides workflows for organizing and examining energy-related inventory information, including electricity, thermal energy and cooling requirements.

The broader development direction includes energy-balance assessment and the relationship between process operating conditions, heating demands and energy recovery opportunities.

### 5.5 Heat integration and resource-efficiency assessment

In many production systems, individual unit operations are assessed independently even though thermal energy may potentially be recovered or exchanged across the system.

LCIAnalysis is being developed to support a more integrated consideration of thermal requirements and opportunities for heat recovery.

This is particularly relevant when exploring prospective industrial configurations, where energy integration can influence the inventory used for environmental assessment.

The scientific reliability of such analyses depends on appropriate thermodynamic properties, process conditions, boundaries and engineering assumptions.

### 5.6 Scientific data quality, provenance and review

Reliable LCA modeling requires more than a collection of numerical values.

Researchers need to understand where data originate, how values were obtained, what assumptions were applied and which information remains uncertain.

LCIAnalysis aims to support this transparency by connecting reported data, source information, calculation review, scientific queries and inventory revisions.

This is important when datasets contain a mixture of experimental measurements, literature values, process simulations and engineering estimates.

### 5.7 Query management and scientific communication

Scientific communication is treated as part of inventory development rather than as an unrelated administrative task.

LCIAnalysis supports structured clarification records to help researchers communicate inventory issues and document responses.

This can make repeated data-review cycles more manageable and help preserve important scientific decisions across the duration of a project.

### 5.8 Scaling and multi-system integration

Emerging technologies are often represented by several datasets developed at different scales or under different assumptions.

LCIAnalysis V2 introduces workflows intended to support reference-basis scaling, integration of process datasets and the representation of connected production systems.

These capabilities can help users examine larger production configurations and multi-product systems, subject to appropriate scientific assumptions and validation.

### 5.9 Scientific reporting and export

Scientific software is most useful when its results can be inspected and shared.

LCIAnalysis supports structured exports intended to communicate inventory data, graphical process representations and calculation results.

Particular emphasis is placed on making detailed mass-balance information accessible in formats suitable for scientific discussion with data providers and project partners.

## 6. Practical applications

### Application A — Developing an inventory for a fermentation-based product

A consortium is developing a new fermentation-derived food ingredient.

Different partners contribute information about raw material preparation, cultivation, fermentation, downstream separation and purification.

The LCA team needs to assemble these datasets into a consistent production system.

LCIAnalysis can support the process by organizing the partner inventories, visualizing unit operations, assessing stream connectivity, checking material balances and documenting questions about missing or inconsistent data.

### Application B — Reviewing a complex industrial inventory

An industrial partner provides a workbook containing many material inputs, outputs and energy flows.

The reported process quantities do not reconcile.

LCIAnalysis helps the practitioner examine the calculations, identify discrepancies and formulate targeted scientific questions rather than manually searching through disconnected spreadsheet formulas.

### Application C — Managing repeated inventory submissions

A technology developer provides successive revisions as experimental results become available.

The LCA team needs to understand which assumptions have changed and whether the revised values affect downstream calculations.

A structured inventory-review and communication workflow helps support this iterative process.

### Application D — Developing an integrated production scenario

Several process datasets represent different parts of a future production system.

The researcher needs to align reference bases, examine process connections and explore system-level integration.

LCIAnalysis V2 provides a development framework for these activities, including multi-system representation and energy-related assessment.

## 8. Relationship to conventional LCA software

LCIAnalysis is intended to complement, rather than replace, established life cycle assessment modeling software.

Conventional LCA platforms focus on constructing product systems, linking inventories to background databases and calculating environmental impacts.

LCIAnalysis concentrates on an earlier and frequently underestimated stage:

**Preparing, reviewing, reconciling and integrating the foreground process inventory.**

Its role is to help researchers improve the structure, consistency, traceability and scientific interpretation of primary inventory data before these datasets are transferred into downstream LCA modeling workflows.

LCIAnalysis does not automatically establish the completeness, representativeness or scientific validity of a dataset, and it should not be presented as an independently validated environmental impact assessment engine.

## 10. Vision

### Toward more transparent, efficient and collaborative life cycle inventory development

The long-term vision of LCIAnalysis is to make the development of life cycle inventories more systematic, transparent and scientifically connected.

As sustainability assessments increasingly depend on multidisciplinary collaboration and complex emerging technologies, LCA practitioners need tools that support not only environmental calculations but also the engineering interpretation and management of the underlying data.

LCIAnalysis seeks to provide this missing connection.

**By bringing together structured data collection, process modeling, mass and energy analysis, scientific communication and system integration, LCIAnalysis aims to reduce the fragmentation of LCI development and strengthen the foundation on which reliable life cycle assessments are built.**
